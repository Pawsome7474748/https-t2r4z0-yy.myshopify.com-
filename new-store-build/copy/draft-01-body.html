It took me eleven eyeliners and about four years to stop believing the front of the tube.

I am particular about my eyeliner.

Not in a makeup artist way. More in a "I will remove my entire eye and start again if the wings don't match" way.

So on this particular Friday I took my time. Phone propped against the mirror. Left eye. Right eye. Tiny adjustment. Check again.

Nine minutes.

Then I went out and forgot about it. That was the whole point. I'd already done my makeup. I was finished.

The photos came the next morning

My friend sent the photos the next morning.

First one, fine. Second one, fine.

Third one.

The wing on one side had softened. There was a small dark smudge near the corner. And under my lower lash line was a faint line of black that definitely wasn't there when I left the house.

I zoomed in. Then out. Then back in.

And I wasn't looking at the photo anymore. I was looking at my eyeliner.

That's the part that got me. Not that it wasn't perfect — that I'd spent the whole night assuming it was fine, and found out from someone else's camera.

You start doing things you don't notice

Once that happens a few times you start doing things you don't even notice.

Checking your reflection in shop windows. Looking at your own face in your phone before someone takes a picture. The bathroom trip halfway through the night for a "quick check." Carrying the liner in your bag just in case.

Taking a photo of yourself before an event, because you want a record of how it looked before it started moving.

I wasn't wearing eyeliner and forgetting about it.

I was wearing eyeliner and monitoring it.

I have a drawer of things that didn't work

And I'd tried to fix it. Properly. I have a drawer.

The cheap waterproof pencil looked lovely going on, then started printing onto my lid every time I blinked, so by eleven I had a soft grey second line sitting above the first one.

The liquid liner stayed sharper for longer. But one wobble and I was removing half my eye and starting again.

The gel I actually liked — and it needed the entire ritual. Primer. Powder. A steady hand. Waiting between layers. And if the night got warm, none of it mattered anyway.

Then I did what everyone does and started taking advice off the internet. Set your lids with powder. More primer. Setting spray. Change the angle. Less product. More product. Different brush.

Some of it bought me an hour.

None of it fixed the actual thing.

Application was never the problem

Because I could always make my eyeliner look good at the start of the night. I knew how to apply it. I'd been doing it for years. Application was never the problem.

The problem was hour four.

After getting warm. After my eyes watered. After blinking, laughing, touching my face, and doing every ordinary thing you do without thinking about it.

That's when the perfect wing quietly became something else.

So I stopped buying them. I'd read "waterproof" and "long wear" enough times to know those words weren't telling me anything about what my face would look like at midnight.

What actually changed

I've been using it since 23/02/26, and the change isn't that my makeup got better. I'm doing the same wing, same mirror, same hands, same nine minutes.

The change is that I stopped carrying a pencil in my bag.

I don't check shop windows anymore. I take the photo first and look at it after. That's a small thing to describe and a strange relief to actually have.
